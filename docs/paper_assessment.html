Paper Assessment: JUST2CE Draft

“The transition to a sustainable economy is not sustainable”

Overall verdict

Strong analytical content. The framing, model, and scenario findings are solid. The paper reads as a Frankenstein because the seams between sections are visible and some foundational pieces are missing or inconsistent. The substance is there — this is a question of finishing the structure and making the register consistent.

What works well

The abstract is probably the best-written part — tight, covers model, findings, and normative implication in the right order.

The introduction is strong. The political-economy framing (not just “does CE work?” but “for whom, through which channels, with what global consequences?”) is well-motivated and the literature engagement is coherent. The four-part research question (i–iv) on page 3 is the clearest statement of purpose in the paper.

The descriptive analytics section is a genuine contribution. The portfolio effectiveness heatmap (Figure 4) is analytically sharp — the key point that the structural factor is approximately constant and therefore channel scale dominates outcomes is well-explained and directly anticipates the simulation results.

The CE Trilemma and Portfolio Scale Principle sections read as the most analytically mature writing in the paper. Dense but well-argued.

Main structural problems

1. The paper has no conclusions

The Conclusions heading is there with nothing under it. The paper currently ends with Policy Implications, which is a strong section but is not a substitute for a conclusion that synthesises, reflects on limitations, and makes the “so what” argument fully.

2. Cut-off sentence on page 4

“We apply this model to analyse” — this sentence ends abruptly and is never completed. It sits right before the results preview paragraphs, creating a gap in the narrative bridge to the findings.

3. The Scenario Analysis opening paragraph is from a different draft

The section opens with “In this exercise we ran 14 alternative shocks connected with CE practices, which are categorized among their domains…” — flat, procedural, reads like an internal methods note. It also contains an incomplete sentence (“Among the CE shocks affecting…”). The rest of the Scenario Analysis — Portfolio Scale Principle, Two Transition Regimes, CE Trilemma — is written in a completely different, more mature register. The opening needs to be replaced with a proper analytical introduction.

4. Figure numbering is broken

  • Page 9: “Figure 1 decomposes gross output”
  • Page 11: “Figure 3: EU Import Structure” (Figure 2 is missing)
  • Leontief decomposition figure: no number (just a subsection heading, not captioned)
  • Page 13: “Figure 4” for the portfolio heatmap
  • Appendix 3 references “Figures 1 and 2” for calibration convergence — conflicts with main text numbering

5. Missing transition from Descriptive Statistics into Scenario Analysis

The descriptive analytics section ends with Figure 5 (CO2 emission heatmap) and then the Scenario Analysis begins with no bridging sentence. There is no paragraph that says “these descriptive patterns anticipate the simulation results — here is how.” That bridge exists implicitly in the portfolio effectiveness heatmap discussion, but it needs to be made explicit.

6. Tracked changes are visible in the export

“Deleted[Marco Passarella]”, “Deleted[José Bruno Ramos Torres Fevereiro]”, formatting comments — all showing in the PDF. Before sharing with anyone, export with “accept all changes” and comments hidden.

Writing style inconsistencies

The paper has at least three distinct registers:

  • Introduction: Flowing, politically-engaged academic prose — well-suited to Ecological Economics
  • Descriptive Statistics: Technical + bullet-point “Key Patterns” lists — reads like internal research notes pasted in; the bullet format is abrupt and not stylistically integrated
  • Scenario Analysis: The analytical sections (Portfolio Scale Principle, CE Trilemma) are the most polished writing in the paper, but the opening paragraph is from an earlier draft

The bullet-point “Key Patterns” format throughout the descriptive section is the most jarring stylistic inconsistency. These should be converted to analytical prose paragraphs for the journal submission — or at minimum integrated more smoothly.

Smaller issues

  • “Carbon Energy” is used as the label in the paper body (page 10: “Carbon Energy (primary: Fossil-Fuel Electricity; secondary: Renewable Electricity)”) while the code was updated to “Energy”. Decide which label to use and make it consistent throughout.
  • The scenario registry table (page 17) renders very poorly — columns are collapsed and text wraps awkwardly. Needs a proper formatted table (see scenario_registry.qmd).
  • The two comparison figures (Fig 8/9) are embedded at a size where they are effectively unreadable in the paper. Given their analytical centrality, they need to be either reproduced at full page width or referenced as supplementary figures with a selected subset shown in the main text.
  • Appendix 1 (Model Equations) is “…” — needs content before submission.
  • Arranz et al. 2026 cited in the introduction — check that it is published/in press.
