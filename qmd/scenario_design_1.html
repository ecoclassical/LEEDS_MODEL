Scenario Design

1. Conceptual Framework

This document formalizes the structural logic of the scenario design used in the model.
All redistribution mechanisms are constructed to preserve accounting identities and maintain structural consistency between:

  1. Production-side sourcing (technical coefficients matrix \(A\))
  2. Demand-side allocation shares (\(\beta, \sigma, \iota, \iota_g\))

The governing parameter is:

\[ \rho \in [0,1] \]

interpreted as the fraction of original allocation from sector from that is redirected to sector to.

This interpretation is applied symmetrically across production and demand.

2. Production-Side Redistribution

Let \(a_{ij}\) denote the technical coefficient matrix.

For a given shock affecting rows from and to, the redistribution rule is:

\[ a'_{from,j} = (1 - \rho)\, a_{from,j} \]

\[ a'_{to,j} = a_{to,j} + \rho\, a_{from,j} \]

This ensures:

\[ a'_{from,j} + a'_{to,j} = a_{from,j} + a_{to,j} \]

Thus:

  • Column totals are preserved.
  • Total intermediate demand intensity is unchanged.
  • Only sourcing structure changes.

This represents a structural sourcing shift, not a scaling of total production requirements.

3. Demand-Side Redistribution

Let \(\delta_k\) represent any final demand share vector:

  • \(\beta\) (household consumption)
  • \(\sigma\) (government consumption)
  • \(\iota\) (private investment)
  • \(\iota_g\) (public investment)

For a redistribution between sectors from and to, the symmetric rule is:

\[ \delta'_{from} = (1 - \rho)\, \delta_{from} \]

\[ \delta'_{to} = \delta_{to} + \rho\, \delta_{from} \]

This preserves the budget simplex:

\[ \sum_k \delta'_k = \sum_k \delta_k \]

No re-normalization is required.

This ensures conceptual symmetry with the production shock.

4. Unified Interpretation of ρ

Under this design, \(\rho\) has a single structural meaning:

Fraction of original allocation from sector from that is redirected to sector to.

It does not represent a final share split between the pair.

It does not rescale total demand.

It operates as a directed redistribution operator.

5. Implementation Notes

5.1 Production Scenarios

The model applies the redistribution directly to the technical coefficient multipliers before simulation.

Key properties:

  • Column sums preserved.
  • No negative coefficients created for \(0 \le \rho \le 1\).
  • Leontief structure remains coherent.

5.2 Demand Scenarios

The function compute_delta_eff() applies redistribution at each period \(t \ge t_{shock}\):

Pseudo-logic:

m_from <- delta[from]
delta[from] <- (1 - rho) * m_from
delta[to]   <- delta[to] + rho * m_from

Exports and imports are excluded from redistribution logic.

6. Economic Interpretation

Production shocks represent:

  • Technological or sourcing transitions
  • Circular economy substitution
  • Material substitution in intermediate demand

Demand shocks represent:

  • Consumption reallocation
  • Investment redirection
  • Public procurement shifts

Because the operator is identical in form, the model ensures structural comparability between technological and behavioural transitions.

7. Stability Considerations

For \(0 \le \rho \le 1\):

  • No coefficient becomes negative.
  • Accounting constraints hold exactly.
  • Redistribution is bounded.
  • Comparative statics remain interpretable.

Values of \(\rho\) above 0.3–0.4 may imply rapid structural change and should be interpreted as aggressive transition scenarios rather than marginal adjustments.

8. Summary

The scenario design now satisfies:

  • Algebraic consistency
  • Accounting preservation
  • Symmetry across production and demand
  • Clear interpretation of the redistribution parameter

This provides a structurally coherent foundation for scenario analysis and policy experimentation.
